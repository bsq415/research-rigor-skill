# Discover an empirical question worth testing

Use for selecting a new empirical research idea, reconstructing how one was
selected, or turning several mature research lines into a concrete study.
This route comes from project selection records, competing scouting reports,
machine-readable pilot decisions, and later corrections. Use the decision
structure; adapt the field, budget and evidence. It does not require inventing
a new algorithm. For a mathematical method transfer, use
[literature to idea](literature-to-idea.md).

## Recover what actually happened

When reproducing a past success, inspect the working directory before trusting
its retrospective. Build a private source map: constraints, original candidate
reports, search/reading artifacts, selection decision, pilot protocol and output,
amendments, full results, manuscript, and review provenance. Save hashes and
line/key anchors. Classify each statement as planned, executed, observed,
researcher-reported, or reconstructed; mark unavailable originals.

Keep parallel scouts and rejected alternatives. Conflicting rankings are
branches, not evidence of a unanimous choice. A document date or embedded commit
label alone does not reconstruct an unavailable event history. A final paper
does not prove that its final contribution was apparent at selection time.

Reconcile reading claims with retained records and actual reading depth. A small
sample neither verifies a larger reported corpus nor proves the larger search
never occurred. Missing task outputs remain missing. Do not promote abstract
annotations to verified full-text reads or reproduce a historical paper quota.

## 1. Set the contribution and resource envelope

Establish the decision to change, intended reader, reusable assets, data/label
access, hardware, time, money and evidence required. Separate a useful question
from a result that is merely possible to compute. If resources change, preserve
the original envelope and amendment instead of rewriting the initial rationale.

Choose a research form that can produce decisive evidence: measurement,
evaluation, critical analysis, theory or a method. A measurement study can be
substantive when it exposes an unresolved decision or invalidates an assumption;
adding more datasets or renaming an existing benchmark is insufficient.

## 2. Map relationships, then generate competing candidates

Use a compact map with these axes:

`practical change | adopted tool | assumed invariant | prior evaluation object |
missing comparison | feasible evidence`.

Read for fast-changing practices, mature tools, saturated questions, contradictory
findings, and conditions that were held fixed. Popularity and venue keywords
help discovery; they do not establish importance or publication probability.
Track source depth and uncertainty using [idea and literature](idea-and-literature.md).

Generate technically distinct questions, including a strong simple alternative.
Keep the survivors and kill reasons together. In a focused request, existing
notes or the optional [selection card](../assets/empirical-idea-card.md) suffice;
do not require the full controller or a universal candidate count.

## 3. Find the assumption at the interface

A productive question is often:

> When practice A changes operating condition X for a real benefit, does tool B,
> validated under Y, still support the same decision? What must remain fixed to
> answer that, and what would a different answer change?

Specify A, B, X, Y, the assumed invariant, the affected actor and consequence.
An unchanged component does not imply unchanged inputs to the component that
monitors or uses it. Investigate that interface rather than just joining two
popular labels. Other bridges are valid; do not force every domain into transfer.

Write a candidate before the verdict: question, exact comparison, decision value,
strongest alternative explanation, and a cheap check. This is a proposed study,
not a fabricated result. Explain the value of opposite, null and mixed outcomes
before seeing them. A study that only matters if its preferred sign appears
needs a stronger question or a different design.

## 4. Try to kill the gap with the closest work

For each serious candidate, search the actual object and causal direction,
including other terminology, earlier methods, negative results and later work.
Inspect consequential primary text, not only titles or a scanner's verdict.

| Compare | Question |
|---|---|
| Question and decision | Does the nearest work already answer the same practical question? |
| Intervention and fixed objects | Is it changing a condition, adapting a tool, or testing a frozen one? |
| Evaluation unit and population | Same object, paired unit, shift, denominator and target population? |
| Evidence and scope | What does its actual result establish, and what meaningful uncertainty remains? |

Record source anchors, overlap, missing evidence and disposition. Kill a covered
candidate, or propose a technically meaningful unresolved comparison. A larger
matrix, extra qualifier, reversed verbal description or new label alone does
not escape prior work. Earlier work can support plausibility without proving
the phenomenon's sign, universality, novelty or likely acceptance.

Keep historical selection distinct from novelty at today's search cutoff.
Reopen a gap when a counterexample appears. A high weighted score cannot rescue
a fatal novelty, validity or feasibility failure.

## 5. Make the new question visible in the experiment

An evaluation object can carry the contribution when established components
cannot answer the question in their usual evaluation arrangement. State what is
new about the question answered, not just the number of rows or columns.

For a transfer question, distinguish:

- fitting/validating a tool separately within each condition;
- freezing its fit and threshold at a source condition, then serving elsewhere;
- adaptation or repair, with its extra labels, tuning and compute accounted for.

A source-condition by destination-condition matrix is useful only if those
cells answer distinct meaningful questions. Compare source-trained and
destination-trained tools on the same held-out examples, with training/tuning
access separated. Retain diagonal controls and simple proxies. A fixed operating
threshold and a newly tuned threshold are different deployment decisions.

Measure the intervention actually realized; a nominal condition label is not
proof of exposure. Separate changed base performance, changed measurement and
changed monitoring utility. Report coverage for each metric and paired subset;
different survivor sets cannot establish a cross-signal mechanism. Do not infer
independence, invariance or equivalence from non-significance.

## 6. Pilot information value and measurement validity separately

Build the smallest end-to-end chain that can change selection: intervention,
raw outputs, labels/parsing, features or fitted tools, matched comparisons,
uncertainty, coverage and a readable decision table. Include the brittle path,
not only the easiest cell. A generation run is not a complete evidence chain.

Predeclare three branches in terms of the candidate's uncertainty:

- **Proceed:** the measurement works and evidence/information value justifies
  further investment under the available budget.
- **Repair or defer:** validity, labels, implementation or access is unresolved;
  preserve affected cells and continue only work supported by valid evidence.
- **Reject or redesign:** the gap is covered, the comparison cannot identify the
  target, or the remaining study cannot change the intended decision.

Preserve the actual rule and authorization used. An operational `continue`
decision can coexist with undefined scientific outcomes; it is not an all-green
certificate. A historical default-to-continue rule is not a universal scientific
admission rule. Unknown evidence cannot establish no effect, no transfer loss
or equivalence. A valid null may be informative when its precision addresses the
decision; an underpowered result or broken measure is not that null.

## 7. Freeze the question; let evidence mature the answer

Freeze the comparison, population, failure rules and resource account before
expansion. Keep the early hypothesis, observed pilot and final contribution
separate. A striking pilot establishes a tested case, not a universal sign.
Broader results may reveal mixed effects, a boundary, or a different mechanism.

Retain the decision-relevant question when it remains valid; version unsupported
claims and label new discoveries exploratory. A substantial question/design
change reopens novelty and evidence checks. Do not rename every unfavorable
result into a successful pivot, discard contrary cells, or keep buying runs
until a preferred headline appears. Repair experiments must answer a specified
failure mechanism; an extra module is not automatically a contribution.

## Deliver a reproducible selection decision

Leave a candidate, its closest alternatives, fixed/changed objects, decisive
comparison, observed/planned evidence, resource limit, disposition and next
action. Use the existing candidate ledger and novelty assessment for a full
freeze; keep a focused ideation request lightweight.

Explain the main value in one sentence and show the experiment that would make
that value inspectable. Preserve source paths and exact unpublished results
privately. Track actual review praise separately from researcher recollection,
author replies and AI simulations. Praise can identify valued attributes; it
does not prove that this workflow caused recognition or guarantees replication
of that outcome.
